I'm a real estate developer, and a lot of my job is figuring out how other people have already done something. Condo offering plans are one of the best places to find out. Before a sponsor can sell condo units in New York, it has to file a plan with the Attorney General, and it's all public: how a building was set up, who the team was, the budget, and what was promised to buyers.

Once, on a condo tour, the guide mentioned that one of the units was an MIH unit, an affordable apartment under the city's Mandatory Inclusionary Housing program, sitting inside a condo. That's exactly what another developer would want to know. But unless you were on that tour, you'd have to open thousands of long PDFs one at a time and hope.

So I built The Condo Book Project to search them all at once. Every result shows the actual language from the filing, the document it came from, and the page it's on, so you can check it yourself.

It doesn't have everything. The Attorney General hasn't posted documents for every filing, and not everything posted has been read yet, so if a search comes up empty, that doesn't mean the thing doesn't exist. The FAQ explains what's covered.
