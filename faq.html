Frequently asked questions

Short answers about what The Condo Book Project covers and how to read a result.

The basics

What is a condo offering plan?

It is the disclosure document a sponsor files with the New York State Attorney General before selling condominium units. It describes the building, the units offered, the prices, the budget, the declaration and bylaws, and the sponsor's obligations. Changes after filing are made through numbered amendments.

What does The Condo Book Project do?

It lets you search the text of filed offering plans and amendments across projects at once, and shows the matching language with the document and page it came from. Every result links back to the Attorney General's record so you can check the original.

Is The Condo Book Project free?

Yes. There is no account and no sign-up.

Is The Condo Book Project part of the Attorney General's office?

No. The Condo Book Project is an independent research tool run by Half Ave Company LLC. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or acting for the Attorney General's office or any government agency.

Searching

How should I phrase a search?

Write it the way you would ask a person, for example Queens condos under 20 units with parking spaces. The Condo Book Project pulls out the borough or neighborhood, the unit count and the parking count as filters, and searches the plan text for the rest. The line under the search box shows how your question was read.

Can I search for an exact phrase, or one word or another?

Yes. Put an exact phrase in double quotes, like "roof rights". Use OR between words to match either one, like doorman OR concierge.

How are results ordered?

When you search for words, plans with the most matching pages come first. When you only filter (for example by borough and unit count), the newest accepted plans come first. Up to 60 plans are shown.

What do the dots on the map mean?

Green dots are plans whose documents can be searched page by page. Outlined dots are plans we only have the Attorney General's summary record for. Orange dots match your current search.

Coverage and accuracy

Why didn't my search find a building I know exists?

The Condo Book Project searches the plans and amendments currently indexed here. The Attorney General has not posted documents for every filing, and some posted documents are not yet indexed. A missing result does not mean a feature or project does not exist.

What does "Later amendments not searched" mean?

The plan has amendments on file that The Condo Book Project has not read yet. A later amendment can change prices, unit counts, amenities, dates and more, so check the latest amendment on the Attorney General's site before relying on what the original plan said.

Does a result mean a unit or parking space is for sale?

No. Plans describe what was offered at the time of filing. They do not establish what is currently available for sale. Check the original filing, relevant amendments, and current project information.

Where do the unit and parking counts come from?

From the Attorney General's plan data. They reflect what the plan record says and may not match the building today.

The quoted text looks garbled. Why?

Many plans are scanned PDFs, and the text is read from the page image. Tables, stamps and handwriting can come through imperfectly. The page reference lets you check the original.

What are the building images?

3D massing drawings made from NYC Building Footprints, with the plan's building in green. They show shape and height, not what the building looks like.

Documents

How do I download a plan?

Press Download plan. It opens the plan's Documents tab on the Attorney General's site, where you tick the files you want and choose Download. The Condo Book Project does not host the PDFs.

The Attorney General hasn't posted documents for a plan. Can I still get them?

Copies can be requested from the Attorney General's office through a Freedom of Information Law (FOIL) request.

Contact

How do I report an error?

Email hello@halfave.co with the plan ID (for example CD260081, shown on each result) and what looks wrong.

I'm a sponsor or owner and want something corrected.

Write to hello@halfave.co. The Condo Book Project shows public filings as the Attorney General posted them, so we can fix how we read or display a document, but the filing itself can only be changed through the Attorney General.
