Short answer: about six to nine months. Across NYC condo offering plans where the Attorney General recorded both a submission date and an acceptance date, the median time from submission to "accepted for filing" is 193 days. A quarter of plans cleared in under 4.5 months; a quarter took more than 9.5 months.

For plans accepted since 2020, the middle is closer to eight months, and the slow tail is longer. For the most recent plans, one by one, see the condo offering plan approval times data page.

What "Approval" Means

The AG doesn't approve a condo. It accepts the offering plan for filing under the Martin Act, meaning the disclosure is complete enough to be shown to buyers. Until then, a sponsor can't take a binding contract or a deposit. The clock starts when the plan is submitted and ends on the acceptance date printed on the AG's record. What Makes an Offering Plan Slower

From plans accepted since 2020:

  • New construction takes longer than conversions. Median 272 days for new buildings vs. 225 for rehabs. New construction plans carry more open items: certificates of occupancy, tax abatement applications, construction budgets.
  • Small buildings aren't faster. Plans with 10 or fewer residential units had a median of 248 days. A 6-unit plan gets the same deficiency letters as a 60-unit plan, and small sponsors often respond more slowly.
  • Borough matters less than the sponsor's counsel. Brooklyn plans (the largest group) ran a median of 230 days. The spread within any borough is far wider than the difference between boroughs. See which firms file NYC condo plans in the offering plan attorneys directory.

Where the Time Actually Goes

The AG's first review typically comes back with a deficiency letter within a few months. Most of the calendar is spent on the sponsor's side: revising the plan, waiting on the architect's description of property, updating the budget, and re-submitting. Two or three rounds is normal. A plan that answers the first letter completely and quickly can close the gap by months.

What to Plan For

  • Budget eight months from submission to first contract.
  • Add time if the building isn't topped out, if a 421-a or other tax program is pending, or if the sponsor hasn't done a plan before.
  • Once accepted, the plan must be declared effective within a set period, or the sponsor files an amendment to extend it. See how to find offering plan amendments.

Common Questions

How long does the NY Attorney General take to accept a condo offering plan?

The median is 193 days from submission to acceptance for filing, a little over six months. For plans accepted since 2020 it is closer to eight months.

Can a sponsor sell condo units before the plan is accepted?

No. Until the Attorney General accepts the offering plan for filing, a sponsor can't take a binding contract or a deposit.

Does new construction take longer to approve than a conversion?

Yes. For plans accepted since 2020, new construction plans had a median of 272 days against 225 for rehabs, because they carry more open items such as certificates of occupancy and tax abatement applications.

Does "accepted for filing" mean the Attorney General approved the building?

No. It means the plan's disclosure is complete enough to be shown to buyers under the Martin Act. It is not an endorsement of the building, the price or the sponsor.
