What "Approval" Means

The AG doesn't approve a condo. It accepts the offering plan for filing under the Martin Act, meaning the disclosure is complete enough to be shown to buyers. Until then, a sponsor can't take a binding contract or a deposit. The clock starts when the plan is submitted and ends on the acceptance date on the AG's record. (More on what the filing contains: what is a condo offering plan.)

Why Only Some Plans Can Be Timed

Most plans on the AG's record can't be measured. Once a plan is amended, the submission date on its record is the amendment's, which usually falls after the plan was accepted. Only plans with no amendment yet still carry their original submission date, so these figures use those plans alone, and only the most recent of them, since review times years ago say little about today's.

Where the Time Actually Goes

The AG's first review typically comes back with a deficiency letter. Most of the calendar is spent on the sponsor's side: revising the plan, waiting on the architect's description of property, updating the budget, and re-submitting. Several rounds are common. A plan that answers the first letter completely and quickly can close the gap by months.

What to Plan For

  • Budget about {{stat:appr_median}} from submission to first contract, and allow for the slow quarter: more than {{stat:appr_q3}}.
  • Add time if the building isn't topped out, if a tax program application is pending, or if the sponsor hasn't done a plan before.
  • Once accepted, the plan must be declared effective within a set period, or the sponsor files an amendment to extend it. See how to find offering plan amendments.

Common Questions

How long does the NY Attorney General take to accept a condo offering plan?

Can a sponsor sell condo units before the plan is accepted?

No. Until the Attorney General accepts the offering plan for filing, a sponsor can't take a binding contract or a deposit.

Does new construction take longer to approve than a rehab?

Among the most recent plans, new construction had a median of {{stat:appr_new_median}} against {{stat:appr_rehab_median}} for rehabs and conversions. New buildings carry more open items, such as certificates of occupancy and tax abatement applications.

Does "accepted for filing" mean the Attorney General approved the building?

No. It means the plan's disclosure is complete enough to be shown to buyers under the Martin Act. It is not an endorsement of the building, the price or the sponsor.
